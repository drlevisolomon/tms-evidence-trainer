# Patient FAQ Claim Draft

These are source-linked claim drafts for technician-delivered patient education. They should be adapted to clinic policy and clinician-approved scripts before release.

## Mechanisms

### What does the magnetic pulse do?

The coil does not put electricity directly through the scalp. It uses a magnetic pulse to induce tiny electrical currents in the targeted brain area.

- Evidence strength: `strong`
- Sources: `33243615; 19833552`
- Boundary: This is a physical mechanism claim, not a guarantee of clinical response.

### Why are treatments repeated over many sessions?

The benefit is usually expected to build over repeated sessions, much like practicing a pattern with the brain rather than flipping an instant switch.

- Evidence strength: `strong`
- Sources: `31901449; 28541649; 27090022`
- Boundary: Individual response timing varies and depends on diagnosis, protocol, and patient factors.

### Why target the DLPFC in depression?

For depression, TMS usually targets a frontal brain network involved in mood regulation, attention, and emotional control.

- Evidence strength: `strong`
- Sources: `28541649; 27090022; 17573044; 31901449`
- Boundary: Exact target selection varies by clinic, device, diagnosis, and protocol.

### Is motor threshold a depression measure?

The thumb or hand twitch test helps set a personalized treatment dose. It is not a test of how depressed you are.

- Evidence strength: `strong`
- Sources: `33243615; 41949547; 42082007`
- Boundary: Some research links excitability metrics to physiology, but clinical dosing use is the main technician-facing point.

### Can behavior make TMS work better?

The best practical advice is consistency: keep your normal caffeine, sleep, and medication routine unless your clinician changes it.

- Evidence strength: `moderate`
- Sources: `33243615; 39756660; 41346487; 36911138; 34872405`
- Boundary: Patient-specific medical advice belongs to the treating clinician.

### Is TMS the same as ECT?

TMS is not ECT. It does not require anesthesia and is not intended to cause a seizure.

- Evidence strength: `strong`
- Sources: `28541649; 27090022; 33243615`
- Boundary: ECT remains an evidence-based treatment for some conditions; avoid stigmatizing comparisons.

## Safety and Side Effects

### What side effects are common?

The most common issues are temporary scalp discomfort or headache. Tell staff if it becomes intense or different from usual.

- Evidence strength: `strong`
- Sources: `33243615; 19833552; 28541649; 27090022`
- Boundary: Side effect profile varies by protocol, coil, target, intensity, and patient history.

### How rare are seizures?

Seizures are rare, but the clinic screens carefully because safety matters.

- Evidence strength: `strong`
- Sources: `33243615; 19833552; 34133991; 9474057`
- Boundary: Risk is not zero and can change with sleep loss, medications, substances, neurological illness, and protocol parameters.

### Why use ear protection?

The earplugs are for the loud clicking sound from the coil.

- Evidence strength: `strong`
- Sources: `33243615; 19833552; 1549231`
- Boundary: Patients with hearing devices or auditory sensitivity may need clinician-specific handling.

### Can TMS trigger mania?

Report unusually elevated mood, decreased need for sleep, impulsivity, agitation, or feeling unlike yourself.

- Evidence strength: `moderate`
- Sources: `33243615; 28541649; 31901449`
- Boundary: Risk assessment is diagnosis- and medication-dependent.

### How should headache be discussed?

Mild headache can happen. Please tell us if it is severe, unusual, or does not settle the way it normally does.

- Evidence strength: `strong`
- Sources: `33243615; 28541649; 27090022`
- Boundary: Do not give individualized medication advice outside scope.

## Patient FAQ

### Why does it feel like tapping?

The tapping or pulsing feeling is expected. It comes from the pulse and nearby scalp muscles/nerves reacting.

- Evidence strength: `strong`
- Sources: `33243615; 19833552`
- Boundary: Painful or unusual sensations still need assessment.

### Should I keep caffeine the same?

Try to have about your usual amount of caffeine each treatment day unless your clinician advises otherwise.

- Evidence strength: `moderate`
- Sources: `41346487; 36911138; 34872405`
- Boundary: Evidence is still emerging and does not define a universal caffeine dose.

### Does sleep matter?

Aim for your usual sleep schedule and tell us if you slept very poorly or stayed up all night.

- Evidence strength: `moderate`
- Sources: `39756660; 33243615`
- Boundary: The evidence does not support a single sleep-hour cutoff for all patients.

### Should I take my medications as usual?

Take your medications the usual way unless your prescriber tells you otherwise, and tell us about any changes.

- Evidence strength: `strong`
- Sources: `33243615; 19833552; 28541649`
- Boundary: Urgent medication questions should be routed to the prescriber.

### What if I miss a session?

Call the clinic if you miss a session. Do not try to double up unless the treatment team specifically schedules it.

- Evidence strength: `clinic-policy-dependent`
- Sources: `28541649; 27090022`
- Boundary: Published guidelines do not replace device- and clinic-specific orders.

### When should I expect improvement?

Some people notice changes early, but many need multiple weeks. Keep reporting symptoms honestly as treatment continues.

- Evidence strength: `strong`
- Sources: `17573044; 28541649; 27090022`
- Boundary: Accelerated protocols may have different response timing.

### Can I drink alcohol or use cannabis during the course?

Tell the clinic about alcohol, cannabis, or other substance changes. The goal is safety and consistent treatment conditions.

- Evidence strength: `strong`
- Sources: `33243615; 19833552`
- Boundary: Specific abstinence rules vary by clinic and diagnosis.

### Is discomfort a sign that treatment is working?

Stronger pain does not mean better treatment. Tell us if it hurts so we can assess it.

- Evidence strength: `strong`
- Sources: `33243615; 28541649`
- Boundary: Some protocols may include ramping or adaptation periods.

### Why do you ask the same questions every day?

The daily questions are a safety check. Your answers help us know whether anything changed since the last session.

- Evidence strength: `strong`
- Sources: `33243615; 11332408`
- Boundary: Exact daily checklist content is clinic-specific.

### What should I report between sessions?

Report anything medically significant or unusual, especially medication changes, fainting, seizure-like events, severe headache, or major mood changes.

- Evidence strength: `strong`
- Sources: `33243615; 19833552; 28541649`
- Boundary: Emergency symptoms require emergency care, not just next-session reporting.

### If I feel better, can I stop early?

Feeling better is important, but talk with the treatment team before changing the treatment schedule.

- Evidence strength: `clinic-policy-dependent`
- Sources: `28541649; 27090022`
- Boundary: Continuation, taper, maintenance, and retreatment protocols vary.

### What if I am anxious during treatment?

It is common to feel nervous at first. Tell us what you feel so we can coach you through what is expected and what needs attention.

- Evidence strength: `limited`
- Sources: `41812418; 42090145`
- Boundary: Expectancy effects are real but should not be used to imply symptoms are imagined.

### Is twitching normal?

Some twitching around the scalp, forehead, or face can be normal. Tell us if it changes, hurts, or feels unusual.

- Evidence strength: `strong`
- Sources: `33243615; 19833552`
- Boundary: New or excessive movement should trigger setup review.

### How does TMS work?

The coil makes a brief magnetic pulse. That pulse passes through the scalp and induces tiny electrical currents in the targeted brain area.

- Evidence strength: `strong`
- Sources: `33243615; 19833552`
- Boundary: Mechanism language does not predict individual response.

### Why do I need so many sessions?

Each session is part of a course. The goal is for effects to build over repeated treatments, so sticking with the schedule matters.

- Evidence strength: `strong`
- Sources: `31901449; 28541649; 27090022`
- Boundary: Course length and schedule depend on the prescribed protocol and clinical response.

### What is motor threshold?

The hand or thumb movement test helps the team set your dose. It is not a test of how depressed you are or whether you are improving.

- Evidence strength: `strong`
- Sources: `33243615; 41949547; 42082007`
- Boundary: RMT methods vary; clinics should follow their device and protocol SOP.

### What side effects are common?

The most common issues are temporary scalp discomfort, headache, twitching near the coil, or fatigue. Tell us if anything is intense, unusual, or worsening.

- Evidence strength: `strong`
- Sources: `33243615; 19833552; 28541649; 27090022`
- Boundary: Common does not mean ignorable; severe or unusual symptoms require escalation.

### How rare are seizures?

Seizures are rare, and that is why we screen carefully and follow safety procedures at every visit.

- Evidence strength: `strong`
- Sources: `33243615; 19833552; 34133991; 9474057`
- Boundary: Risk depends on protocol, device, history, medications, sleep, substances, and other factors.

### Why do I need ear protection?

The earplugs are for the clicking sound from the coil. They help protect your hearing during treatment.

- Evidence strength: `strong`
- Sources: `33243615; 19833552; 1549231`
- Boundary: Patients with hearing devices, tinnitus, or ear symptoms may need individualized review.

### Can TMS trigger mania or feeling too activated?

Tell us if you feel unusually energized, need much less sleep, feel impulsive, agitated, or unlike yourself.

- Evidence strength: `moderate`
- Sources: `33243615; 28541649; 31901449`
- Boundary: Assessment depends on diagnosis, medication plan, and clinician judgment.

### Is TMS the same as ECT?

TMS is not ECT. You stay awake, it does not require anesthesia, and the treatment is not intended to cause a seizure.

- Evidence strength: `strong`
- Sources: `28541649; 27090022; 33243615`
- Boundary: ECT is an evidence-based treatment in appropriate clinical situations; do not frame it as inferior or unsafe.

### What if I have an implant or metal?

Tell us about any implants, devices, metal, shrapnel, aneurysm clips, stimulators, or hearing devices. The clinical team will review whether treatment is safe.

- Evidence strength: `strong`
- Sources: `33243615; 19833552; 9803953; 9918361`
- Boundary: Compatibility decisions may require device manuals, manufacturer information, imaging history, or specialist input.

### What about pregnancy, age, or complex medical situations?

These situations do not always mean TMS is impossible, but they do mean the clinician needs to review your specific case.

- Evidence strength: `strong`
- Sources: `33243615; 19833552; 40865772; 40429604`
- Boundary: Evidence differs by subgroup, diagnosis, protocol, and risk profile.

### What if I get a headache?

Mild headache can happen. Please tell us if it is severe, unusual for you, getting worse, or not settling the way it normally does.

- Evidence strength: `strong`
- Sources: `33243615; 28541649; 27090022`
- Boundary: Do not give individualized medication advice outside scope.

### Will I need maintenance or another course later?

Some patients discuss maintenance or another course later, but that plan depends on your response, relapse risk, symptoms, coverage, and clinician judgment.

- Evidence strength: `moderate`
- Sources: `28541649; 27090022`
- Boundary: Maintenance frequency, duration, coverage, and evidence differ by clinic and indication.

## Protocol Literacy

### What protocols are best established for depression?

For depression, TMS usually uses a studied prefrontal protocol repeated across a treatment course.

- Evidence strength: `strong`
- Sources: `17573044; 28541649; 27090022; 31901449`
- Boundary: Guideline support varies by indication and protocol.

### How does iTBS compare with 10 Hz rTMS for depression?

Some shorter TMS protocols have evidence comparable to longer standard sessions for depression.

- Evidence strength: `strong`
- Sources: `29726344`
- Boundary: Non-inferiority was shown for a defined population and protocol.

### Can depression evidence be applied to OCD, smoking, migraine, pain, or other conditions?

TMS is studied for several conditions, but each condition has its own evidence and protocol.

- Evidence strength: `strong`
- Sources: `31901449; 33243615`
- Boundary: Regulatory clearance can differ from guideline evidence and from local clinical practice.

### How should placebo and expectancy be discussed?

Expectations can affect how treatment feels, but TMS also has protocol-specific biological effects being studied and used clinically.

- Evidence strength: `moderate`
- Sources: `42090145; 28541649; 17573044`
- Boundary: Placebo-control designs differ across protocols and devices.
