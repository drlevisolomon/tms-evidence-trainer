# Technician Workflow Claim Draft

These claims are intended for SOPs, checklists, and technician education. They are not a substitute for device manuals, prescriber orders, or local emergency policy.

## Mechanisms

### What does the magnetic pulse do?

- Claim: A TMS coil carries a brief current pulse that creates a changing magnetic field, which induces an electric field in nearby brain tissue.
- Technician detail: Dose at cortex depends on coil type, location, orientation, intensity, scalp-to-cortex distance, and anatomy.
- Training action: Use this wording to explain basic mechanism without implying direct electrical injection.
- Evidence strength: `strong`
- Sources: `33243615; 19833552`
- Boundary: This is a physical mechanism claim, not a guarantee of clinical response.

### Why are treatments repeated over many sessions?

- Claim: Repeated TMS sessions are intended to produce cumulative changes in cortical excitability and network function, not just a transient sensation during the session.
- Technician detail: Avoid promising same-day improvement; treatment effects can emerge gradually and vary across protocols and patients.
- Training action: Frame adherence as part of cumulative dosing.
- Evidence strength: `strong`
- Sources: `31901449; 28541649; 27090022`
- Boundary: Individual response timing varies and depends on diagnosis, protocol, and patient factors.

### What is theta burst stimulation?

- Claim: Theta burst stimulation uses patterned bursts of pulses designed to produce lasting excitability changes with shorter stimulation times than many conventional rTMS protocols.
- Technician detail: Classic motor-cortex studies found iTBS and cTBS can shift excitability in different directions, but individual responses are variable.
- Training action: Teach iTBS/cTBS as protocol-specific patterns, not interchangeable abbreviations.
- Evidence strength: `moderate`
- Sources: `15664172; 18566003; 21221011`
- Boundary: Motor-cortex physiology does not translate perfectly to every psychiatric target.

### Why target the DLPFC in depression?

- Claim: Depression protocols commonly target prefrontal regions, especially left DLPFC, because these regions participate in mood-relevant cognitive-control and limbic networks.
- Technician detail: Do not describe DLPFC as a single depression switch. It is a network target with protocol- and localization-dependent effects.
- Training action: Use network language in patient education.
- Evidence strength: `strong`
- Sources: `28541649; 27090022; 17573044; 31901449`
- Boundary: Exact target selection varies by clinic, device, diagnosis, and protocol.

### Is motor threshold a depression measure?

- Claim: Resting motor threshold is a calibration measure of motor-cortex excitability used to scale stimulation intensity, not a measure of mood severity.
- Technician detail: RMT should be documented as a dosing calibration variable with method and muscle/site details.
- Training action: Correct patient misconceptions during mapping.
- Evidence strength: `strong`
- Sources: `33243615; 41949547; 42082007`
- Boundary: Some research links excitability metrics to physiology, but clinical dosing use is the main technician-facing point.

### Why is brain state consistency emphasized?

- Claim: TMS effects are state-dependent: sleep, caffeine, medications, cortical excitability, attention, and time of day can alter neurophysiological responses.
- Technician detail: Record meaningful state changes and notify the prescriber when changes could affect safety, tolerability, or dosing.
- Training action: Ask about major changes before sessions and document them.
- Evidence strength: `moderate`
- Sources: `37738899; 39756660; 41346487; 36911138; 34872405`
- Boundary: Evidence is stronger for neurophysiologic variability than for simple rules that guarantee better clinical response.

### Can behavior make TMS work better?

- Claim: Consistent sleep, caffeine, medications, and substance use can reduce avoidable variability, but evidence does not support promising that any single behavior will make TMS work.
- Technician detail: Avoid coaching patients to increase caffeine, skip medicines, or change sleep to chase a stronger effect.
- Training action: Use consistency language rather than optimization claims.
- Evidence strength: `moderate`
- Sources: `33243615; 39756660; 41346487; 36911138; 34872405`
- Boundary: Patient-specific medical advice belongs to the treating clinician.

### How should we discuss biomarkers?

- Claim: EEG, qEEG, fNIRS, and TMS-EEG biomarkers are promising but should be presented as emerging tools unless locally validated for a defined clinical decision.
- Technician detail: Label biomarker workflows as research, exploratory, or validated according to the clinic's actual protocol and evidence.
- Training action: Prevent overinterpretation of qEEG/fNIRS readouts.
- Evidence strength: `moderate`
- Sources: `41483580; 42066923; 42055012`
- Boundary: Specific devices or algorithms may have local validation not captured by broad PubMed screening.

### What does personalized targeting add?

- Claim: Personalized targeting and electric-field-informed workflows can improve anatomical or circuit precision, but they require additional imaging, modeling, QA, and interpretation infrastructure.
- Technician detail: Do not treat personalized, neuronavigated, robotic, or circuit-guided targeting as a simple coordinate substitution.
- Training action: Create separate competencies for MRI-guided and circuit-guided workflows.
- Evidence strength: `moderate`
- Sources: `42204340; 42044040; 41438652`
- Boundary: Precision workflows do not automatically prove superior outcomes in every setting.

### Is TMS the same as ECT?

- Claim: TMS is a nonconvulsive outpatient neuromodulation technique and is mechanistically and procedurally distinct from electroconvulsive therapy.
- Technician detail: Use this distinction when patients arrive with fear based on ECT assumptions.
- Training action: Include a TMS-versus-ECT misconception item in onboarding.
- Evidence strength: `strong`
- Sources: `28541649; 27090022; 33243615`
- Boundary: ECT remains an evidence-based treatment for some conditions; avoid stigmatizing comparisons.

## Safety and Side Effects

### What side effects are common?

- Claim: The common side effects of TMS are usually local and transient, especially scalp discomfort, headache, facial muscle twitching, and fatigue.
- Technician detail: Track side effect type, intensity, timing, protocol changes, and whether intervention was needed.
- Training action: Normalize expected sensations while keeping adverse-event reporting active.
- Evidence strength: `strong`
- Sources: `33243615; 19833552; 28541649; 27090022`
- Boundary: Side effect profile varies by protocol, coil, target, intensity, and patient history.

### How rare are seizures?

- Claim: TMS-associated seizure is rare when modern safety guidelines and screening are followed, but it is the key serious acute risk technicians must be prepared for.
- Technician detail: Know the clinic seizure-response procedure, stop stimulation immediately, protect the patient, and summon medical help.
- Training action: Use screening and emergency readiness as core competencies.
- Evidence strength: `strong`
- Sources: `33243615; 19833552; 34133991; 9474057`
- Boundary: Risk is not zero and can change with sleep loss, medications, substances, neurological illness, and protocol parameters.

### Why use ear protection?

- Claim: Ear protection is recommended because the coil click can be loud, even though permanent hearing injury is not expected when appropriate precautions are used.
- Technician detail: Confirm hearing protection before each session and document exceptions according to policy.
- Training action: Make ear protection a non-negotiable setup step.
- Evidence strength: `strong`
- Sources: `33243615; 19833552; 1549231`
- Boundary: Patients with hearing devices or auditory sensitivity may need clinician-specific handling.

### What must be screened before treatment?

- Claim: TMS screening should cover seizure history, neurological illness, implanted or ferromagnetic material, electronic devices, pregnancy status where relevant, medication/substance changes, and prior adverse reactions.
- Technician detail: Use the clinic's formal checklist and escalate changes rather than making independent clearance decisions.
- Training action: Run an update screen at baseline and before sessions as local policy requires.
- Evidence strength: `strong`
- Sources: `33243615; 19833552; 11332408`
- Boundary: Final eligibility decisions are clinician responsibilities.

### What symptoms require escalation?

- Claim: Seizure, loss of consciousness, syncope, chest pain, new neurological symptoms, severe unusual headache, mania/hypomania symptoms, or persistent confusion require immediate escalation.
- Technician detail: Stop stimulation and follow emergency or prescriber-notification policy; document objective observations.
- Training action: Build escalation triggers into technician training.
- Evidence strength: `strong`
- Sources: `33243615; 19833552; 28541649`
- Boundary: Clinics should adapt wording to local medical protocols.

### Are theta burst protocols safe?

- Claim: Theta burst stimulation has a safety literature, but safety depends on protocol parameters, patient selection, and adherence to updated guidelines.
- Technician detail: Verify exact iTBS/cTBS protocol, intensity, train structure, pulse count, and contraindication screening.
- Training action: Treat TBS setup as protocol-specific, not as a generic shorter rTMS.
- Evidence strength: `moderate`
- Sources: `21221011; 33243615; 29726344`
- Boundary: Older safety reviews predate some accelerated protocols.

### How should medication changes be handled?

- Claim: Medication changes can affect seizure threshold, cortical excitability, tolerability, and possibly clinical response, so technicians should document changes and alert the treating clinician.
- Technician detail: Pay special attention to benzodiazepines, anticonvulsants, stimulants, substances, and medications known to affect seizure threshold.
- Training action: Create a medication-change escalation rule.
- Evidence strength: `strong`
- Sources: `33243615; 19833552; 38299320; 31368628`
- Boundary: Evidence on benzodiazepines and efficacy is mixed; safety review is separate from efficacy speculation.

### Can TMS trigger mania?

- Claim: Mood switching or mania/hypomania is uncommon but clinically important, especially in bipolar-spectrum illness or when symptoms change rapidly.
- Technician detail: Escalate suspected manic or mixed symptoms to the treating clinician before continuing treatment.
- Training action: Include mania symptom prompts in safety training.
- Evidence strength: `moderate`
- Sources: `33243615; 28541649; 31901449`
- Boundary: Risk assessment is diagnosis- and medication-dependent.

### How should pregnancy or special populations be handled?

- Claim: Pregnancy, adolescence, older age, epilepsy, implanted devices, and complex neurological or psychiatric histories require clinician-level review rather than technician-level clearance.
- Technician detail: Do not reassure or exclude independently; escalate to the prescriber or medical director.
- Training action: Build special-population flags into intake and daily screening.
- Evidence strength: `strong`
- Sources: `33243615; 19833552; 40865772; 40429604`
- Boundary: Evidence differs substantially by subgroup and indication.

### How should implanted devices be handled?

- Claim: Implanted electronic devices and ferromagnetic or conductive material near the stimulation path are safety-review triggers because magnetic fields can interact with hardware.
- Technician detail: Document device type, location, manufacturer information if available, and clinician clearance.
- Training action: Maintain an implant-escalation checklist.
- Evidence strength: `strong`
- Sources: `33243615; 19833552; 9803953; 9918361`
- Boundary: Compatibility decisions may require device manuals or manufacturer consultation.

### How should headache be discussed?

- Claim: Headache after TMS is usually transient, but severe, unusual, or persistent headache should be escalated rather than dismissed.
- Technician detail: Offer only clinic-approved comfort measures and route medication questions to clinical staff.
- Training action: Add headache severity and timing fields to adverse-event documentation.
- Evidence strength: `strong`
- Sources: `33243615; 28541649; 27090022`
- Boundary: Do not give individualized medication advice outside scope.

### Why track postmarketing and adverse-event signals?

- Claim: Postmarketing adverse-event surveillance can identify real-world safety patterns that may not be fully visible in trials.
- Technician detail: Adverse-event documentation should be structured enough to support internal QA and required reporting pathways.
- Training action: Train technicians on what counts as reportable internally.
- Evidence strength: `moderate`
- Sources: `41678387; 34133991`
- Boundary: Spontaneous-report databases have reporting bias and cannot estimate incidence alone.

## Technician Workflow

### What must be verified before each session?

- Claim: Before each session, technicians should verify patient identity, treatment order, site/target, protocol, intensity, session number, contraindication updates, ear protection, and adverse events since last session.
- Technician detail: Use a forced checklist rather than memory for setup-critical steps.
- Training action: Convert to a pre-session checklist.
- Evidence strength: `strong`
- Sources: `33243615; 28541649; 27090022`
- Boundary: Device-specific checks should be added from manufacturer manuals.

### What should be documented?

- Claim: Each treatment record should document target/localization method, coil orientation, intensity, motor threshold basis, pulse/train parameters, patient state changes, tolerability, and adverse events.
- Technician detail: Documentation is part of dose fidelity, not clerical afterthought.
- Training action: Audit charts for dose-fidelity fields.
- Evidence strength: `strong`
- Sources: `33243615; 28541649; 27090022`
- Boundary: Exact fields vary by device and clinic.

### How should RMT be measured?

- Claim: RMT should be measured with a defined, reproducible method and sufficient observations, with the method documented so later changes can be interpreted.
- Technician detail: Methods include Rossini-Rothwell-style thresholding, adaptive threshold hunting, and emerging automated tools; clinics should validate their method.
- Training action: Train technicians to one clinic-standard MT method before independent operation.
- Evidence strength: `strong`
- Sources: `41949547; 42082007; 33243615`
- Boundary: Visual twitch detection and EMG-based methods may differ.

### When should RMT be rechecked?

- Claim: RMT should be rechecked according to clinic protocol and when meaningful clinical-state changes occur, such as medication changes, major sleep deprivation, neurological events, long treatment gaps, or unexpected tolerability/motor findings.
- Technician detail: Escalate possible RMT-shifting changes rather than silently continuing the old dose.
- Training action: Define RMT recheck triggers in the SOP.
- Evidence strength: `moderate`
- Sources: `33243615; 37738899; 41949547`
- Boundary: Published evidence does not define one universal recheck interval.

### Does time of day matter?

- Claim: Time of day can influence resting motor threshold in clinical practice, so consistent scheduling may reduce avoidable variability when feasible.
- Technician detail: Do not delay clinically needed care solely for time matching, but note large schedule shifts if MT or tolerability changes.
- Training action: Record schedule changes during troubleshooting.
- Evidence strength: `limited`
- Sources: `37738899`
- Boundary: Time-of-day effects are not yet a universal dosing rule.

### How should targeting methods be taught?

- Claim: Scalp-based targeting, Beam F3, 5 cm/5.5 cm rules, neuronavigation, and circuit-guided methods are not interchangeable; each has assumptions and documentation requirements.
- Technician detail: If the clinic changes targeting method, the chart should preserve which method was used and why.
- Training action: Train target localization as a named workflow, not a general scalp location.
- Evidence strength: `strong`
- Sources: `32289680; 37714408; 33243615`
- Boundary: Clinical superiority between practical targeting methods may be context-specific.

### How does Beam F3 compare with 5.5 cm targeting?

- Claim: Studies comparing Beam F3 and 5.5 cm targeting in depression suggest similar effectiveness in some clinical contexts, but reliability and patient anatomy remain important.
- Technician detail: Do not mix targeting rules mid-course unless directed and documented.
- Training action: Use one target method consistently within a course unless ordered otherwise.
- Evidence strength: `moderate`
- Sources: `32289680; 37714408`
- Boundary: This does not prove all targeting methods are equivalent.

### Why does coil orientation matter?

- Claim: Coil position and orientation affect induced electric-field direction and stimulation of intended tissue, so drift and rotation are dose-fidelity problems.
- Technician detail: Use marks, neuronavigation, fixtures, and repeated checks according to device and clinic workflow.
- Training action: Include coil drift checks during treatment.
- Evidence strength: `strong`
- Sources: `33243615; 19833552`
- Boundary: Exact tolerances depend on target, device, and localization method.

### Can technicians change intensity for comfort?

- Claim: Technicians should use only protocol-approved comfort adjustments and should not independently change prescribed treatment dose or protocol.
- Technician detail: Escalate persistent intolerance; document any ramping, pauses, or clinician-approved changes.
- Training action: Define allowed comfort adjustments in SOP.
- Evidence strength: `strong`
- Sources: `28541649; 27090022; 33243615`
- Boundary: Local scope-of-practice rules control who may adjust what.

### How should a seizure be handled?

- Claim: If a seizure occurs, stimulation should stop immediately and staff should follow emergency protocol focused on patient safety and rapid medical evaluation.
- Technician detail: Protect from injury, time the event, avoid putting anything in the mouth, and summon medical help per protocol.
- Training action: Drill seizure-response workflow.
- Evidence strength: `strong`
- Sources: `33243615; 19833552; 34133991`
- Boundary: Emergency instructions must match local medical governance.

### What is the technician scope boundary?

- Claim: Technicians can execute, monitor, document, and escalate, but diagnosis, eligibility, protocol selection, and medication decisions belong to qualified clinicians.
- Technician detail: Use scripted escalation instead of improvising clinical advice.
- Training action: Make scope boundaries explicit in competency training.
- Evidence strength: `strong`
- Sources: `28541649; 27090022; 33243615`
- Boundary: Specific legal scope varies by jurisdiction and clinic policy.

### How should device-specific training be handled?

- Claim: Device, coil, helmet, neuronavigation, and software workflows require device-specific training in addition to general TMS knowledge.
- Technician detail: Maintain device-specific checkoffs for startup, calibration, coil/helmet placement, fault handling, and shutdown.
- Training action: Separate general TMS education from device competency.
- Evidence strength: `strong`
- Sources: `33243615; 27090022`
- Boundary: Manufacturer manuals and local SOPs are required evidence sources beyond PubMed.

### How should suicidality be handled during a depression course?

- Claim: Worsening suicidality or emergent acute risk should be escalated immediately through the clinic's psychiatric safety pathway.
- Technician detail: Do not wait for the next rating-scale interval when acute risk is disclosed.
- Training action: Integrate suicide-risk escalation into technician scripts.
- Evidence strength: `strong`
- Sources: `28541649; 27090022; 41819354`
- Boundary: Emergency response must follow local law and clinic policy.

## Protocol Literacy

### What protocols are best established for depression?

- Claim: High-frequency left prefrontal rTMS and several related prefrontal protocols have established evidence for major depressive disorder, with protocol-specific indications and parameters.
- Technician detail: Confirm indication, target, frequency, intensity, train structure, pulses, and total sessions against the written order.
- Training action: Teach protocol literacy before device operation.
- Evidence strength: `strong`
- Sources: `17573044; 28541649; 27090022; 31901449`
- Boundary: Guideline support varies by indication and protocol.

### How does iTBS compare with 10 Hz rTMS for depression?

- Claim: The THREE-D trial found intermittent theta burst stimulation was non-inferior to standard high-frequency rTMS for treatment-resistant depression, with much shorter sessions.
- Technician detail: Shorter session length does not mean lower need for exact targeting, dose, and safety checks.
- Training action: Teach iTBS as evidence-based but protocol-specific.
- Evidence strength: `strong`
- Sources: `29726344`
- Boundary: Non-inferiority was shown for a defined population and protocol.

### Are low-frequency right-sided and bilateral protocols evidence-based?

- Claim: Low-frequency right prefrontal and bilateral protocols have supporting evidence, but strength and parameters differ from standard left-sided high-frequency protocols.
- Technician detail: Do not generalize setup parameters from one protocol to another.
- Training action: Separate protocol cards by frequency/side/target.
- Evidence strength: `strong`
- Sources: `31901449; 28541649; 27090022`
- Boundary: Local payer and device indications may be narrower than the literature.

### What about accelerated protocols and SNT/SAINT?

- Claim: Accelerated and Stanford Neuromodulation Therapy-style protocols show promising rapid effects in selected studies, but they require exact protocol fidelity and should not be improvised from standard rTMS.
- Technician detail: Check intersession interval, pulse dose, target method, daily session count, and safety monitoring.
- Training action: Label accelerated protocols as separate workflows.
- Evidence strength: `moderate`
- Sources: `32252538; 34711062`
- Boundary: Evidence base, regulatory status, and clinic readiness vary.

### Are deep TMS and figure-8 rTMS interchangeable?

- Claim: Deep TMS and figure-8 coil rTMS use different coil designs, field distributions, protocols, and device-specific evidence; they are not interchangeable at the technician level.
- Technician detail: Follow device-specific training, safety procedures, helmet/coil positioning, and protocol parameters.
- Training action: Maintain separate competency checklists by device.
- Evidence strength: `strong`
- Sources: `31901449; 27090022; 33243615`
- Boundary: Device-specific manuals and FDA labeling should be added to the evidence base.

### Can depression evidence be applied to OCD, smoking, migraine, pain, or other conditions?

- Claim: Evidence and cleared indications differ by condition; technicians should not generalize depression protocol claims to other diagnoses.
- Technician detail: Use condition-specific protocol orders and patient education language.
- Training action: Separate diagnosis-specific modules.
- Evidence strength: `strong`
- Sources: `31901449; 33243615`
- Boundary: Regulatory clearance can differ from guideline evidence and from local clinical practice.

### Why are dose variables not convenience variables?

- Claim: Frequency, train duration, intertrain interval, intensity, pulse count, target, and session spacing are active treatment variables.
- Technician detail: Do not alter pulse count, spacing, or intensity to fit scheduling unless a clinician-approved protocol allows it.
- Training action: Build hard stops for protocol deviations.
- Evidence strength: `strong`
- Sources: `33243615; 9474057; 31901449`
- Boundary: Some devices automate these fields; technicians still need to verify them.

### How should clinical outcomes be tracked?

- Claim: Treatment response should be tracked with validated clinical measures and clinician assessment, not technician intuition alone.
- Technician detail: Administer scales exactly as assigned and escalate suicidality or deterioration immediately.
- Training action: Integrate scale due dates into workflow.
- Evidence strength: `strong`
- Sources: `28541649; 27090022`
- Boundary: Scale choice is clinic- and diagnosis-specific.

### What about maintenance or retreatment?

- Claim: Maintenance and retreatment approaches exist, but they are less standardized than acute-course protocols and should be clinician-directed.
- Technician detail: Do not imply a universal maintenance schedule; document relapse, response, and clinician orders.
- Training action: Create a separate maintenance/retreatment module.
- Evidence strength: `moderate`
- Sources: `28541649; 27090022`
- Boundary: Coverage and evidence differ by diagnosis and setting.

### How should special-age populations be taught?

- Claim: Youth and late-life depression TMS evidence is growing, but protocols and safety considerations should be handled as special-population workflows.
- Technician detail: Escalate cognitive impairment, frailty, guardian/consent issues, or pediatric considerations.
- Training action: Add special-population branching in training.
- Evidence strength: `moderate`
- Sources: `40865772; 40429604; 28541649`
- Boundary: Evidence from general adult MDD should not be applied automatically.

### How should placebo and expectancy be discussed?

- Claim: Expectancy and placebo-related effects can influence mood and treatment experience, but they do not make TMS 'just placebo'.
- Technician detail: Use balanced language: optimistic, accurate, and free of guaranteed outcomes.
- Training action: Train neutral expectation-setting.
- Evidence strength: `moderate`
- Sources: `42090145; 28541649; 17573044`
- Boundary: Placebo-control designs differ across protocols and devices.

### How should FDA clearance and literature evidence be separated?

- Claim: Regulatory clearance, payer coverage, guideline support, and research evidence are related but distinct categories.
- Technician detail: Training content should label each protocol as clinic-approved, guideline-supported, FDA-cleared where applicable, or investigational.
- Training action: Add evidence-status labels to protocol cards.
- Evidence strength: `strong`
- Sources: `31901449; 28541649; 33243615`
- Boundary: Regulatory status must be verified from FDA/device sources, not PubMed alone.

## Future Expansion: fNIRS, EEG, qEEG, TMS-EEG

### What can fNIRS add?

- Claim: fNIRS can measure cortical hemodynamic signals during or around stimulation, but TMS/fNIRS workflows remain emerging and protocol-specific.
- Technician detail: Optode placement, hair, motion, ambient light, and timing relative to stimulation can affect signal quality.
- Training action: Pilot fNIRS as a research/QA workflow before clinical claims.
- Evidence strength: `limited`
- Sources: `41478613; 42055949`
- Boundary: Evidence is not yet sufficient for universal response prediction.

### What can EEG or qEEG add?

- Claim: EEG/qEEG may support precision-medicine research for noninvasive brain stimulation in depression, but biomarker-guided decisions remain an emerging area.
- Technician detail: Training must cover electrode quality, artifact management, state control, and who is qualified to interpret outputs.
- Training action: Avoid deterministic qEEG marketing language.
- Evidence strength: `moderate`
- Sources: `41483580`
- Boundary: Algorithm-specific validation is needed before clinical decision use.

### What is TMS-EEG?

- Claim: TMS-EEG measures EEG responses to TMS pulses and can probe cortical reactivity, but stimulation artifacts and preprocessing choices are major technical challenges.
- Technician detail: Cap setup, electrode impedance, amplifier protection, artifact correction, timing, and patient movement are core workflow burdens.
- Training action: Treat TMS-EEG as a separate competency.
- Evidence strength: `moderate`
- Sources: `42055012; 42066923; 41344323`
- Boundary: Outputs need expert interpretation and validated preprocessing.

### How should measurement tools be introduced into the platform?

- Claim: Measurement tools should be organized by readiness level: clinic-ready workflow, clinic-QA tool, research-use biomarker, or investigational method.
- Technician detail: Each measurement module should state setup burden, artifact risks, interpretation authority, and clinical decision pathway.
- Training action: Add readiness labels before expanding into qEEG/fNIRS modules.
- Evidence strength: `strong`
- Sources: `41483580; 42055012; 42066923`
- Boundary: Readiness may change as local validation and new evidence accumulate.

### What is the main artifact risk in TMS-EEG?

- Claim: TMS-EEG is vulnerable to large pulse-related, muscle, auditory, somatosensory, and equipment artifacts that can dominate the measured signal if not controlled.
- Technician detail: Artifact control starts before analysis: cap preparation, coil positioning, hearing masking/ear protection, impedance, and motion control matter.
- Training action: Build a TMS-EEG artifact checklist.
- Evidence strength: `moderate`
- Sources: `42055012; 42066923`
- Boundary: Preprocessing decisions can change interpretation.

### Who should interpret qEEG or biomarker outputs?

- Claim: Biomarker outputs should be interpreted by qualified clinicians or researchers within a validated workflow, not by technicians from raw displays alone.
- Technician detail: Technician responsibilities should focus on high-quality acquisition, artifact notes, and escalation of technical issues.
- Training action: Define acquisition versus interpretation roles.
- Evidence strength: `strong`
- Sources: `41483580; 33243615`
- Boundary: Local credentialing and device claims may vary.

### What makes fNIRS quality fragile?

- Claim: fNIRS signal quality can be affected by optode contact, hair, motion, superficial blood flow, ambient light, and task timing.
- Technician detail: Training should include optode placement, signal-quality checks, motion notes, and exclusion criteria.
- Training action: Do fNIRS dry runs before using it in a treatment workflow.
- Evidence strength: `limited`
- Sources: `41478613; 42055949`
- Boundary: TMS/fNIRS evidence in psychiatric treatment is still early.

### How should data privacy be handled for added measurement tools?

- Claim: Adding EEG, qEEG, fNIRS, imaging, or biomarker data increases privacy, storage, and interpretation responsibilities beyond routine treatment logs.
- Technician detail: Know consent status, file naming, storage location, access limits, and whether data are clinical or research records.
- Training action: Add data-governance training before multimodal expansion.
- Evidence strength: `clinic-policy-dependent`
- Sources: `33243615`
- Boundary: This requires local privacy/legal policy beyond PubMed evidence.

### When should multimodal measurement be added?

- Claim: Multimodal measurement should be added only when there is a defined clinical, QA, or research question and a plan for acting on or interpreting the data.
- Technician detail: Every added modality needs a decision pathway, artifact policy, and responsible interpreter.
- Training action: Require a modality purpose statement in future modules.
- Evidence strength: `strong`
- Sources: `41483580; 42055012; 42066923`
- Boundary: Exploratory research protocols can collect data without immediate clinical action if consented and governed appropriately.
